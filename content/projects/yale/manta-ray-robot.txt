## The idea
DIVA (Deepwater Investigation & Visual Analysis) is a manta-ray-inspired underwater glider that flies on buoyancy instead of propellers. Our team built it as a senior design capstone. The motivation is simple: according to the US National Ocean Service, 75% of the seafloor is still unmapped, and most underwater vehicles spend much of their energy fighting drag with propellers. DIVA is a QNX-sponsored project.

## How it moves
The DIVA robot harnesses the highly-efficient biomechanics of the manta ray, utilizing its oscillating and undulating wing motion to move quietly and while conserving energy.

## My part
Our team of four split the work by discipline. Murat Khidoyatov covers mechatronics and controls, Nowyshin Mridula handles manufacturing, and Chloe DeJoy works on soft robotics and controls. I'm on mechanics and CAD, where I:

- Research and design the exterior structures of the glider.
- Perform structural and hydrodynamic calculations.
- Apply aerodynamic principles and Ansys Fluent to optimize the hull.
- Build the SolidWorks models and technical drawings.

Our team also compared materials for the dorsal canopy, weighing rigid printed PLA and resin against waterjet aluminum.

## The plan
The schedule runs from Fall 2026 to Spring 2027:

- Fall: requirements, a decision matrix, and CAD and fluid simulation.
- December: detail drawings, fin molds and a manufacturing plan.
- January: hull fabrication, assembly and leak tests.
- February: electronics and control loop integration, and a first pool test.
- March: tuning the trim and fin waves, and reconciling simulation with pool data.
- April to May: final validation, demo videos and the report.
